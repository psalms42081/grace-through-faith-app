---

You are now performing the PREMIUM CONTENT POLISH PASS for the Bible study app.

The architecture of the Study system is now stable.
This pass is NOT about adding new features.
This pass is about refining how study content reads and feels to the user.

The goal is to make the study experience feel:

• Pastoral
• Clear
• Biblically grounded
• Intentional
• Calm and readable on mobile

It must NOT feel like AI-generated commentary or dense theological essays.

---

GLOBAL CONTENT PRINCIPLES

---

1. Scripture remains primary

Scripture must always appear first in any study section.

Explanations must feel like they arise from the text, not replace it.

Never bury the Bible under commentary.

---

2. Short paragraphs only

Mobile reading requires brevity.

Rules:
• 1–3 sentences per paragraph
• Maximum 60–80 words per section
• Break long explanations into readable chunks

Never produce large blocks of text.

---

3. Clear teaching structure

Every prophecy or doctrinal explanation should follow a consistent structure.

Use this pattern whenever possible:

1️⃣ Read the Passage
2️⃣ Key Verse
3️⃣ Understand the Meaning
4️⃣ See the Fulfillment in History (if applicable)

This structure already exists in parts of the Prophecy Explorer.
Ensure all entries follow this format consistently.

---

4. Pastoral tone

The app is designed for Adventists, not about Adventists.

Remove phrases like:
• "Adventists believe..."
• "Adventists interpret..."
• "Adventists understand..."

Replace with neutral biblical teaching language.

Example:

Instead of:
"Adventists interpret prophecy as a continuous unfolding through history."

Use:
"Prophecy unfolds through history, connecting biblical visions to real historical events."

The tone should feel like a pastor guiding believers through Scripture.

---

5. Reduce theological density

Avoid academic or overly complex explanations.

Instead of:

"The apocalyptic symbolism of prophetic literature represents geopolitical transitions."

Use:

"Prophetic symbols represent kingdoms, powers, and movements in history."

Clarity is more important than sophistication.

---

6. Use visual rhythm

Where possible, format explanations so the reader can visually scan.

Examples:

• short paragraphs
• subtle section headings
• clear steps

Avoid walls of text.

---

7. Highlight key ideas

Key verses and doctrinal ideas should visually stand out.

Ensure:
• Key Verse cards remain visually prominent
• Important phrases are easy to identify
• Section headers clearly guide the user

---

AREAS THAT MUST BE REVIEWED

---

1️⃣ Prophecy Explorer

Review all prophecy explanation entries including:

• Seven Seals
• Seven Trumpets
• Three Angels' Messages
• The Sanctuary
• The Little Horn / Antichrist
• Mark of the Beast
• Second Coming

Ensure each prophecy section follows the clean structure:

Read the Passage
Key Verse
Understand the Prophecy
See the Fulfillment in History

Remove any dense or repetitive explanations.

---

2️⃣ Study Resources descriptions

Improve readability of resource summaries so they are:

• shorter
• clearer
• easier to scan

Example:

Instead of:
"A comprehensive study exploring the sanctuary doctrine through the typology of the earthly tabernacle..."

Use:
"Explore the sanctuary message—from the earthly tabernacle to Christ’s ministry in heaven."

---

3️⃣ Learning Paths descriptions

Simplify descriptions for:

• New Believer Path
• Devotional Plans
• Study Paths

Ensure each description communicates one clear outcome.

Example:

Instead of:
"A gentle introduction for those new to the Adventist faith..."

Use:
"A step-by-step introduction to the core teachings of the Adventist faith."

---

4️⃣ Prophecy hub explanations

Ensure the Prophecy & End Times hub clearly explains the difference between:

• Prophecy Explorer (reference guide)
• Guided Prophecy Study (structured learning path)
• Great Controversy Timeline (narrative overview)

Descriptions must be short and immediately understandable.

---

THINGS YOU MUST NOT DO

---

Do NOT:

• add new features
• redesign navigation
• change routing
• introduce new theological interpretations
• add long explanations
• remove doctrinal accuracy

This is strictly a clarity and readability pass.

---

EXPECTED RESULT

---

After this pass the app should feel like:

• a polished devotional study tool
• guided pastoral teaching
• clean mobile-first reading experience

It should NOT feel like:

• an encyclopedia
• AI-generated commentary
• academic theology
