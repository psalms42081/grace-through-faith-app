These are genuinely impressive. The visual design is well above what most indie apps ship with. Let me give you specific, honest feedback on each screen.

**Study Scripture screen (image 1)**

The image cards are doing exactly what you intended — they communicate depth and reverence instantly. The Renaissance-style artwork is distinctive and consistent. Two things to fix:

The header says "study-category" in plain text at the top. That's a route name leaking into the UI. It should either be hidden or replaced with nothing — the "Study Scripture" heading below it already serves as the page title. Having both creates a redundant and unprofessional double-header.

The back chevron (`<`) sitting to the left of "Study Scripture" is slightly awkward — it's competing with the browser back button at the top. On native this would feel cleaner. On web it creates two competing navigation affordances at different vertical positions.

**Home screen top (image 2)**

This is strong. "Good morning / Grace Through Faith / Read · Reflect · Pray · Practice · Grow" is a clean, purposeful greeting. The Daily Rhythm section with Read/Study/Pray as sequential cards is excellent UX — it gives a new user an immediate answer to "what do I do first?" The Verse of the Day card with the Renaissance painting is visually the best thing on the screen.

One concern: "Read Proverbs 3" and "Study" and "Pray" are all image cards of similar weight. A new user can't tell which one is most important or where to start. The visual hierarchy is flat. Consider making "Read Proverbs 3" slightly more prominent — larger, or with a subtle accent border — since that's the entry point to everything else.

**Home screen bottom (image 3)**

The Sabbath School card with "Continue Your Journey / Lesson 12 · Living With..." is well executed. The Lesson Companion card in purple beneath it is nicely differentiated.

The Daily Formation rings at 0% are the most important thing to address on this screen. Three concentric rings all at zero with "0%" in the center is the first thing a new user sees below the fold — and it communicates "you haven't done anything yet" in a way that feels deflating rather than motivating. The tooltip "Scripture renews the mind. Read a chapter to grow your study ring" is trying to help but the empty rings dominate. Consider either not showing the rings until the user has completed at least one action, or replacing the 0% with something like "Start today" to make it feel like an invitation rather than a score.

The "Share Feedback" card at the very bottom is fine but it's prime real estate. Once you have more content (upcoming devotional day, recent study, etc.) that should replace it.

Overall: the visual language is cohesive and premium. The two things that would most improve first impressions are fixing the route name leak in the header and rethinking the empty formation rings. Send more screens when you're ready.
